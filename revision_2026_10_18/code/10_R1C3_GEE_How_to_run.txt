RUN THIS FILE
10_R1C3_GEE_Small_Chips.js

1. Open https://code.earthengine.google.com/ in your browser and select your
   existing authorized Earth Engine project. No project ID is hardcoded.
2. Create a new script and paste the ENTIRE JavaScript file, including the
   CHIP_DEFINITIONS at the top. Save it as R1C3_Small_Chips.
3. Click Run. The map displays the ten chip outlines and the Console shows
   small previews. Only those chips are processed; the map's country-scale
   view does not mean country-wide imagery processing.
4. Open Tasks. Five CSV export tasks appear. Click Run for each task.
   They export tables only, not satellite imagery. Wait until tasks finish.
5. Retrieve the five CSV files from Google Drive folder Adnan_R1C3_GEE_Chips
   and share them for interpretation. If a task fails, retain its exact error.

SMALL TEST OPTION
Set CFG.maxChips to 1 for a first quick test, then use 10 for the full intended
pilot. Disable individual run... flags to run modules separately if needed.
There is no all-Pakistan imagery export or full 1985-2025 reconstruction.

DESIGN
Ten small chips: two per elevation band, selected reproducibly from the exact
existing NDVI grid. Each is one original grid-cell footprint, approximately
0.05 degrees (about 5 km across; actual dimensions vary with latitude).
Chip_manifest.csv records cell IDs, coordinates, elevation and selection fields.

Selection uses seed 20261014, elevation-band stratification, broad 2014 land
cover and spatial separation. Where available, irrigated cropland is paired
with natural vegetation; not every elevation band contains both. Selection
does not depend on suppression, recovery or agreement between sensors.
These purposive chips are a targeted test, not a representative probability
sample of the entire country or a complete confounder-adjusted experiment.

Main comparison years:
2001: Landsat 5 versus Landsat 7.
2014: Landsat 7 versus Landsat 8.
2022: Landsat 8 versus Landsat 9.
Monthly sensor checks use January, April, July and October only.
Seasonal profiles use twelve MODIS months in each of these three years.
Five individual source-probe months are 1999-04, 2000-07, 2005-07, 2014-07,
and 2022-07. These are small additional probes, not full annual reconstructions.

THE FIVE OUTPUT TABLES
01_covariates.csv (30 rows): annual MODIS land-cover classes and chip fractions
for forest, shrub/savanna, grass, crops, mixed crop/natural vegetation,
snow/ice and barren land. Also includes a static 2010 irrigation/rainfed
cropland proxy from GFSAD1000. Cell_id/year allows linking to R event records.

02_sensor_P75_checks.csv (120 rows): sensor-specific pixelwise monthly P75,
QA-screened clear-observation counts and agreement on shared valid 30m pixels.
Reports bias B-A, median difference, MAE, RMSE, descriptive spatial correlation
and coverage. At least two clear observations per pixel, 100 common pixels
and 20 percent common area are required for reported agreement statistics.
These are coverage rules, not automatic sensor-equivalence acceptance margins.

03_seasonal_profiles.csv (360 rows): monthly QA-screened MODIS NDVI, observation
counts, valid area and an exploratory active-greenness indicator. The indicator
requires >=9 observed months and amplitude >=0.05, then uses monthly NDVI
above annual minimum +20 percent of amplitude. It is a descriptive greenness
proxy, not a validated planting/harvest calendar or field vegetation type.
The 16-day composites are assigned by their start dates.

04_source_fingerprint.csv (300 rows): selected local NDVI values, embedded from
the existing NetCDF, versus Landsat and MODIS candidate products and
aggregation methods. Native-point comparisons and chip means are identified
separately. Errors may reflect source, QA, temporal aggregation, sampling,
projection or spatial aggregation. Correlation or one small error alone cannot
identify a product. Only repeated agreement plus metadata/code provenance can
support identification. Local NA remains blank, not zero.

05_near_time_scene_pairs.csv (120 rows): closest available Landsat scene pair
within each selected month, at most eight days apart and with matching WRS
path/row. Uses shared QA-valid 30m support and the same coverage requirements.
Scene IDs and actual date differences are recorded. Single-scene checks do
not require the monthly two-observation rule. Near-time comparisons reduce
but do not eliminate phenological and acquisition-condition differences.

Some rows will be unassessable, especially winter mountain chips. That is a
real finding about observation coverage. Empty rows or blank statistics must
not be interpreted as perfect sensor agreement or no vegetation response.

QUALITY AND MISSING DATA
Landsat Collection 2 Level 2 reflectances use scale 0.0000275 and offset -0.2.
QA masks exclude fill, dilated cloud, cloud, cloud shadow, snow and saturation;
Landsat 8/9 cirrus is also screened. NDVI is explicit arithmetic with a
nonzero-denominator guard and [-1,1] range check. Missing data are never filled.
No retrospective harmonization coefficient is applied to the existing stack.
Zeros in observation-count or area-count calculations are counts only.

SOURCE WORKFLOW
The repository includes the original Landsat P75 export script. A revised
copy under upstream/ disables short-gap filling. Actual export identifiers
and subsequent grid preparation are needed to link exports to prepared R
inputs. The chip comparisons assess selected sites and processing choices;
they do not establish complete national or full-record sensor equivalence.

WHAT THIS PILOT CAN AND CANNOT ESTABLISH
It can quantify selected sensor/composite differences and show whether chip
land cover, irrigation proxies and observed seasonal patterns covary with
elevation. It cannot prove full-record consistency, historical irrigation
stability, species-level vegetation differences or an independent causal
effect of elevation. Thousands of pixels in one chip are not independent
replicates. Spatial correlations are descriptive, not equivalence tests.

After exports are reviewed, join relevant covariates by cell_id/year/month to
the existing primary R event records. Evaluate within-cover comparisons and
common support before adjusting resistance/recovery models. Do not use an
event's own post-event NDVI to define a causal growing-season control; use an
independent or pre-event seasonal reference for an adjusted analysis. The
current-year seasonal-profile export is primarily a diagnostic.

The manuscript reports the completed earlier pilot. The current optional source
comparison has six candidates per chip-month; the other modules and their
coverage rules are unchanged. This revision does not rerun or alter prior results.
